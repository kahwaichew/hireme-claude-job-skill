# Writing guide: cover letters and emails

## Ground rules

- **Facts only from what you know about the user.** Never invent numbers, employers, tools or achievements. If a strong point needs a missing detail, write the best letter you can without it and ask about it afterwards.
- **Pick content by relevance to this job.** Choose the two or three experiences or achievements that best match the job's top requirements. Don't include interview-style stories (hardest problem, conflicts, failures) unless they directly prove a key requirement.
- **Mirror the job description's wording** for skills the user really has, so recruiters and applicant tracking systems spot the match. Don't keyword-stuff.
- **Sound like a person.** Avoid clichés and AI tells such as "I am writing to express my interest", "I am thrilled", "passionate about leveraging", "dynamic team", "fast-paced environment", "proven track record", "delve" and "synergy". Use plain verbs and short sentences.
- **Handle gaps quietly.** Bridge a gap with a transferable skill in one sentence, or leave it out. Never apologise for missing requirements.
- Use the hiring manager's or company's name if known; otherwise use a neutral greeting that suits the country (e.g. "Dear Hiring Team").
- Don't include salary, notice period or visa status unless the job post asks or the user says to.

## Cover letter (default output)

250 to 350 words, 3 to 4 short paragraphs:

1. **Opening:** the role, plus the user's single strongest match for it.
2. **Proof:** two or three relevant achievements or responsibilities mapped to the top requirements, with numbers where the user gave them.
3. **Why this role or company:** one or two sentences that connect the job description to the user's direction.
4. **Close:** a simple call to action and thanks, then the user's name and contact line.

## Application email (when asked, or when the post says to apply by email)

120 to 200 words. Subject: `Application for {Role}: {Full name}` (use the reference number if the post gives one). Greeting, the role, two or three sentences on the strongest fit, a mention of the attached resume, availability for a call, and a sign-off with contact details.

## Other messages (only when asked)

- **Recruiter / LinkedIn message:** 50 to 100 words. Who they are, the role, one proof point, a polite ask.
- **Follow-up after applying:** under 120 words. Restate interest and add one new relevant point if possible.
- **Thank-you after an interview:** under 120 words. Reference something specific from the conversation.

## Local conventions

Follow the norms of the country where the job is based. For example, US letters are short and direct and leave out personal details (age, marital status, photo), while some European and Asian markets expect a slightly more formal tone. If the job post states what to include (salary expectations, availability, a reference number), include it.
